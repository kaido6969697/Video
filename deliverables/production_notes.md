# Production notes — The $27M Scam Behind a Fake-Funeral Sting

## Format

- 16:9, 1280×720, 24 fps; approximately 3:39.
- English narration generated with one consistent selected narrator voice.
- Original vector-like 2D illustrations are drawn by `create_video.py` with Pillow; generic round-head puppets are not likenesses of real people.
- The background score is synthesized in code; no source music or stock footage is used.
- Fonts are included with their licenses in `fonts/` so the renderer is self-contained.
- Sidecar captions: `fake_funeral_27m_scam.srt` (timings are estimated from paragraph and speech-chunk durations).

## Fact-check boundaries

The U.S. Department of Justice says Jiandong Chen pleaded guilty in April 2026 and was sentenced on November 13, 2026 to 78 months (six and a half years). It describes a wider scheme with more than 2,000 elderly victims and over $27 million in losses during approximately 2021–2023. The DOJ release says Chen admitted losses during his role exceeded $9.5 million. The video deliberately distinguishes the wider scheme’s $27 million from Chen’s admitted $9.5 million-plus period of involvement.

The DOJ describes the fake-funeral YouTube encounter as one attempted cash pickup and separately describes investigators’ other evidence. The narration explicitly avoids claiming that the video alone proved the case or caused the prosecution. The DOJ’s April release also states that the network used remote-desktop software in some scams, fictitious recipient names and fake IDs for package pickups, and cryptocurrency to move proceeds.

The final anti-scam checklist is general safety advice, not legal or financial advice.
